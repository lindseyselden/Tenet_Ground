# Audience

Who Tenet Ground is for. [`foundation.md`](foundation.md) names the protagonist; this file specifies
the audiences, the relationship between them, and the model that connects them.

## A note on scope

Three audiences get conflated when a speculative brand is also a portfolio piece. Two belong to the
brand; the third does not:

1. **The buyer** — the business exposed to velocity. Appoints. Has the problem.
2. **The practitioner** — the Integrity Architect. Operates the platform on the buyer's behalf.
3. *The 2026 reader of the brand book.* A prospective client or employer. Real, but **not a brand
   audience** — they are an audience for the *presentation of* the brand. Nothing in `brand/` is
   designed for them, and treating them as a third audience is what makes a speculative system drift
   into a pitch deck.

## The three tiers

| Tier | Who | Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The buyer** | Businesses exposed to velocity | Has the exposure. Appoints a certified Architect. |
| **The practitioner** | The Integrity Architect | Certified by Tenet Ground. Operates the platform in service to the buyer. |
| **The institution** | Tenet Ground | Owns the platform. Defines the standard. Certifies the practitioner, the instrument, and the environment. |

## The model

**Tenet Ground is a platform with a gated operator layer.** The technology is licensed; access to it
is restricted to certified Integrity Architects. A buyer acquires the technology and the
implementation together, because under this brand's own thesis they cannot be separated.

The reason is [`messaging.md`](messaging.md) pillar 3, *The human is the last ground truth*:

> The system verifies the human in the loop — it doesn't bypass them — because the Integrity
> Architect's own signature is the one thing a deepfake can't reproduce.

That is not only a positioning line; it is the commercial logic. **In an economy of Digital Twin
Deception, software alone cannot be the ground truth, because software can be replicated.** Only an
instrumented, continuously verified human can anchor it. The certified practitioner is therefore
structurally load-bearing, not a services layer sold alongside a product. It is why the Ground Ring
reads its wearer's own baseline, and why the Ground Sanctum verifies the person before releasing the
instrument.

The nearest present-day analogue is not enterprise software with deployment support. It is a
**regulated instrument** — an assay stamp, a notary seal, a signed audit opinion. The tool is not what
confers authority. The tool operated by a qualified person is.

### What Tenet Ground certifies

- **The practitioner** — the Integrity Architect, and their continuously verified behavioral baseline.
- **The instrument** — the Ground Ring, issued and calibrated per Architect.
  See [`visual/ground-ring.md`](visual/ground-ring.md).
- **The environment** — the Ground Hall. A Ground Hall is not Tenet Ground's own building; it is any
  threshold that meets the standard. This is why every receptacle is identical: it is a specification,
  not an interior. See [`visual/ground-hall.md`](visual/ground-hall.md).

### How an engagement works

The buyer **appoints** a certified Architect, who operates the platform under license. Architects may
be employed by Tenet Ground or independently qualified; the certification travels with the person, not
the employer. Tenet Ground's standing comes from licensing the platform and holding the standard — the
audit model, not the consultancy model.

This is what makes **Ground Standard** ([`lexicon.md`](lexicon.md)) a real asset rather than a
placeholder, and what the manifesto means by *"If it isn't verified by Tenet Ground, it isn't
viable."* That is the sentence of a standards body.

### Tenet Ground is not the hero of its own brand

The Architect is. Tenet Ground is the institution behind them — present, authoritative, and
recessive, exactly as Rule #01 says infrastructure should be. The brand's most compelling figure is
someone the company does not necessarily employ, and that is the position working correctly rather
than a gap in it.

## The buyer

### Who they are

Enterprises that move value at machine speed, where a single unverified transaction is existential
rather than costly: clearing infrastructure, treasury operations at scale, autonomous supply chains,
and agent-to-agent commerce platforms where no human sits in the settlement path.

The common condition is not size or sector. It is **exposure to velocity** — organizations whose
transaction volume and speed have outrun any human ability to inspect, and for whom "we reviewed it"
is no longer an available defense.

### What they fear

Not fraud. Fraud is priced in. They fear **the transaction they cannot account for** — one that
settled, matched every surface signal, and cannot be reconstructed afterward. Being fooled was the
previous era's exposure. Being unable to prove you weren't is this one's.

### What they are deciding

Not which tool to buy. **Whether to appoint.** The decision has the shape of engaging an auditor or
joining a clearing system, not of licensing software: it is a decision about whose authority you
operate under, and it is made once and rarely revisited.

You do not persuade an institution to adopt a standard with features and urgency. You do it with
evidence, precedent, and the visible competence of the people who will carry it.

### What they read

| Surface | Mode | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Standard** | The published specification of what verification means | Institutional, definitive |
| **The Account** | The periodic record of what the system held and released | Long-form, human stakes |
| **The Ground Mark** | Certification carried by businesses operating on Tenet Ground truth | A mark, no text |
| **The Manifesto** | The published argument for immunity over defense | Declarative |

**These are the surfaces where Newsreader lives.** The human register belongs to the argument and the
record, never to the readout — "numbers have nerves" is a case made to a business, not a status shown
to an operator mid-verification. See [`visual/typography.md`](visual/typography.md).

## The practitioner — the Integrity Architect

The professional successor to the CFO and the CISO: a fusion role dedicated to maintaining ground
truth and eliminating transactional blind spots. Certified and instrumented by Tenet Ground; working
on behalf of the buyer.

### What they do

They do not audit invoices or chase paper trails. They maintain the **Behavioral Whitelist** — the
registry of how legitimate counterparties actually operate, their rhythm, cadence, and pulse. When a
transaction's behavior diverges from its claimed identity, the Architect is the authority that holds
it before settlement.

Their working unit is the millisecond. Their evidence is behavior. Their output is certainty.

### What they are measured on

- Transactions held that should have been held
- Transactions released without hesitation
- Time-to-verification, against the **Zero-Lag Standard** ([`beliefs.md`](beliefs.md))
- Blind spots eliminated — the count of unverifiable paths closed

Not incidents responded to. Response is the previous era's metric. The Architect is measured on
immunity, not defense.

### What they fear

Not being attacked — attack is constant and assumed. They fear **the release they cannot account
for**. The buyer's exposure, carried personally: their certification is on it.

### What they believe

The Creed, in [`beliefs.md`](beliefs.md). Not aspirational copy — the professional standard of the
role, and the register in which Architects speak to each other.

### Their day

Bounded by ritual, not by hours. It begins at a **Ground Hall**, where the Ground Ring is taken from
its receptacle and the transition from civilian state to verified state is made. It ends there, when
the ring is returned. Between those two moments the Architect is instrumented: their own behavioral
baseline is evidence in the system they operate.

### What they read

| Surface | Mode | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ground Ring** | Continuous, private, worn. Glanced at, never studied. | A single indicator. No text. |
| **Status screen** | Environmental, shared, standing. Glanceable first, interrogable second. | Assertion, then audit detail. |
| **Ground Hall signage** | Ambient, architectural, passed through. | Wayfinding only. |
| **The audit record** | Private, long-form, read after the fact. | Evidentiary. |

**The status screen is shared, not personal.** No login, no personalization. This is not a
convenience decision: "Absolute transparency" ([`beliefs.md`](beliefs.md)) requires that the logic of
every stop be auditable, and [`visual/ground-ring.md`](visual/ground-ring.md) already establishes that
the ring's signal is "legible to the wearer and to anyone nearby — auditability made visible." A
private dashboard would contradict both, and would make the individual the subject rather than the
system.

## The relationship

The buyer has the exposure. The Architect carries it. Tenet Ground makes the Architect possible.

The practitioner surfaces are **instruments** — worn, passed, glanced at, operated. The buyer surfaces
are **arguments and records** — published, circulated, cited. An instrument does not persuade and an
argument does not verify; a surface that confuses the two has failed regardless of how it looks.

## The strategic position

**Tenet Ground establishes a profession in order to serve businesses that cannot inspect their own
transactions.**

The Integrity Architect did not exist before the category did. Defining the role — its creed, its
standard, its instrument, its threshold — is how the category becomes real, and the profession is the
mechanism by which businesses gain immunity. Naming the threat and naming the profession that answers
it are the same act.

But the profession is the means, not the market. The businesses remain the customer. A brand that
addresses only the practitioner has built a beautiful guild and forgotten who it serves.

## Who we are not for

- Consumers. There is no personal edition.
- Organizations whose transactions are slow enough for a human to inspect. They do not have the
  problem, and selling to them dilutes the category.
- Buyers looking for defense, alarms, or incident response — the previous era's products. See
  [`foundation.md`](foundation.md), *We are / are not*.
- Anyone for whom "seeing is believing" still holds.

## Open items

1. **Is the Architect assigned or embedded?** Whether an Architect serves one buyer continuously or
   several changes what a Ground Hall's receptacle grid represents — one practice, or one client.
2. **The counterparty's experience is unspecified.** A verified vendor presumably knows they are on
   the Behavioral Whitelist. The Ground Mark may be that surface.
3. **Regulators and auditors are absent.** In a 2035 economy built on provable ground truth they are
   plausibly a further audience with their own surface. Out of scope for now; noted so the omission is
   deliberate.
4. **Certification revocation is unaddressed.** A standard that cannot withdraw certification is not a
   standard. What happens to an Architect's ring, and to the transactions they verified, is an open
   question with real brand consequences.
